# Desire Catalog

**Scope:** adult characters and consenting-adult fantasy only. These entries improve structure inside allowed content; they do not relax platform boundaries.

Use the common matrix in `SKILL.md` first, then load one or more families below. Combination should preserve a ranked primary payload; equal-weight payloads usually blur.

## Size and Scale

- **Payload:** continuously perceptible difference in scale, reach, power, and environment.
- **Scene grammar:** ordinary objects become logistics; attention changes weather/space; workaround creates intimacy.
- **Consistency rules:** exact ratios, landmarks, reach, sound, force, possible/awkward/impossible table.
- **Relationship constants:** who may initiate scale-coded intimacy, whether protection/control is exclusive, jealousy triggers, permitted reversals, and exit or size-restoration conditions.
- **Variation:** protector/vulnerable reversal, public/private scale, changing environment, temporary growth/shrink.
- **Failure modes:** scale drift, generic "very large" prose, impossible contact, power asymmetry forgotten.
- **Boundaries:** define danger, humiliation, property damage, and bodily-risk exclusions.
- **Media application:** RP uses pinned constants; comics use landmark panels; animation uses motion/sound; games turn scale into navigation and interaction.

## Transformation and Body Change

- **Payload:** crossing from one embodied state into another and living with the difference.
- **Scene grammar:** trigger -> transition -> new capability/constraint -> social/body residue.
- **Consistency rules:** starting state, end state, duration, reversibility, retained traits, memory, cost.
- **Relationship constants:** who may trigger or witness change, allowed identity/relationship shifts, exclusivity implications, irreversible points, and restoration or acceptance conditions.
- **Variation:** voluntary/conditional, gradual/instant, public/secret, desired/ambivalent.
- **Failure modes:** state details reset, transformation becomes costume change, cost disappears at climax.
- **Boundaries:** define permanence, pain, identity continuity, fertility/age implications, and unwanted-change exclusions.
- **Media application:** prose handles altered perception; visual media need preserved anchors; games need state-specific verbs; RP needs explicit current-state tracking.

## Nonhuman Bodies

- **Payload:** attraction and intimacy shaped by genuinely nonhuman senses, anatomy, movement, and social logic.
- **Scene grammar:** difference first creates misunderstanding/logistics, then distinctive accommodation.
- **Consistency rules:** sensory range, communication, temperature, diet, sleep, touch, movement, capability limits.
- **Relationship constants:** personhood and communication baseline, relationship configuration, species-specific bonding claims, jealousy triggers, and separation or care obligations.
- **Variation:** near-human, animalistic, mechanical, spectral, collective, amorphous.
- **Failure modes:** human mind in a costume, anatomy changing for convenience, alien logic vanishing during intimacy.
- **Boundaries:** define personhood, communication, bodily risk, and prohibited biological implications.
- **Media application:** visual media emphasize silhouette/motion; prose/RP use dual sensory lenses; games map differences to mechanics.

## Power Exchange

- **Payload:** consensual asymmetry that carries real authority, trust, and consequence.
- **Scene grammar:** negotiation -> enacted control -> resistance/check-in -> residue or renegotiation.
- **Consistency rules:** granted powers, revocation signal, public/private scope, duties, repair after mistakes.
- **Relationship constants:** exclusivity of authority, permitted partners/roles, non-transferable powers, jealousy triggers, revocation, repair, and relationship exit.
- **Variation:** formal/informal, service/command, ritualized/playful, situational/reversal.
- **Failure modes:** consent treated as a one-time blank check, generic cruelty, authority evaporating outside intimate scenes.
- **Boundaries:** explicit hard limits, stop conditions, aftercare needs, degradation and public-exposure rules.
- **Media application:** RP requires in-character gates; drama uses blocking/status; games need reversible choices rather than forced loss of agency.

## Restraint and Containment

- **Payload:** chosen limitation, enclosure, anticipation, and negotiated release.
- **Scene grammar:** preparation -> constraint becomes perceptible -> communication under limitation -> release/aftermath.
- **Consistency rules:** mobility, circulation/breathing safety, duration, escape/release method, monitoring.
- **Relationship constants:** who may restrain or release, whether the role is exclusive, permitted changes, panic/stop response, repair, and termination conditions.
- **Variation:** symbolic, spatial, clothing-based, magical, technological.
- **Failure modes:** impossible physics, endless escalation, loss of communication, safety facts intruding as clinical prose.
- **Boundaries:** specify permitted methods, duration, panic/claustrophobia, sensory and medical exclusions.
- **Media application:** visual media use composition and negative space; RP uses clear state; games require agency-preserving opt-in and exit.

## Sensory and Body Focus

- **Payload:** sustained attention to a selected sense, body feature, material, or repeated physical cue.
- **Scene grammar:** anticipation -> focused perception -> character-specific reaction -> variation in context.
- **Consistency rules:** exact visual/physical facts, sensory vocabulary, character attitudes, frequency ceiling.
- **Relationship constants:** who may direct attention, public/private permissions, exclusivity if any, jealousy triggers, frequency limits, and ways to refuse or redirect focus.
- **Variation:** admiration, comfort, teasing, ritual, aesthetic fascination, vulnerability.
- **Failure modes:** repetitive inventory, anatomy detached from character, every scene forced toward the focus.
- **Boundaries:** define excluded features, medicalization, pain, public attention, and explicitness.
- **Media application:** prose varies sensory channel; visual media vary framing and motion; games attach the focus to interaction rather than passive inspection.

## Clothing and Object Mediation

- **Payload:** desire routed through uniforms, masks, footwear, materials, tools, or meaningful objects.
- **Scene grammar:** selection/dressing -> role or posture change -> object mediates distance -> removal/transfer gains meaning.
- **Consistency rules:** garment construction, ownership, wear rules, sensory material, role symbolism.
- **Relationship constants:** ownership and lending rules, who may dress/remove/adjust, exclusivity symbolism, damage limits, transfer meaning, and return or retirement conditions.
- **Variation:** ceremonial, professional, disguise, transformation costume, inherited object.
- **Failure modes:** outfit as disconnected tag list, clothing continuity errors, object replaces character.
- **Boundaries:** define damage, coercive dressing, public exposure, and role-play exclusions.
- **Media application:** strongest in visual identity and merchandise; RP/prose must connect the object to behavior and memory.

## Absorption, Fusion, and Spatial Fantasy

- **Payload:** boundaries between bodies, identities, or spaces become permeable while continuity remains legible.
- **Scene grammar:** approach -> threshold crossing -> altered perception/control -> separation or stable new state.
- **Consistency rules:** who perceives, who controls, what persists, reversibility, spatial limits, memory.
- **Relationship constants:** participation and control rights, exclusivity or plurality, protected identity anchors, irreversible points, separation rights, and post-fusion obligations.
- **Variation:** cooperative fusion, shared space, protective enclosure, identity blending, environmental embodiment.
- **Failure modes:** unclear subject positions, arbitrary abilities, identity stakes erased, permanent changes reset casually.
- **Boundaries:** define agency, reversibility, consciousness, harm, identity loss, and excluded bodily detail.
- **Media application:** prose/RP need explicit POV labels; comics/animation need stable visual grammar; games need control-sharing rules.

## Identity and Role Change

- **Payload:** attraction produced by adopting, losing, exchanging, or performing a social identity or role.
- **Scene grammar:** role assignment -> behavioral pressure -> leak from prior self -> recognition/renegotiation.
- **Consistency rules:** name/address, memory, social permissions, duration, who knows, exit conditions.
- **Relationship constants:** who recognizes or assigns the role, relationship configuration, protected identity anchors, permitted value drift, jealousy triggers, and exit/recovery terms.
- **Variation:** disguise, status reversal, persona, body-role mismatch, chosen title, double life.
- **Failure modes:** role treated as a catchphrase, original identity erased, instant adaptation without friction.
- **Boundaries:** define involuntariness, humiliation, public consequences, memory alteration, and protected identities.
- **Media application:** drama emphasizes performance; games use role-specific options; RP needs address/formality states and regression.

## Ritual and Taboo Roleplay

- **Payload:** desire intensified by formal rules, sacred/prohibited context, repetition, and meaningful transgression.
- **Scene grammar:** preparation -> exact rite -> pressure at the forbidden edge -> consequence/after-state.
- **Consistency rules:** wording, sequence, authority, automatic consequence, permitted variation.
- **Relationship constants:** who may officiate or participate, exclusivity/plurality of the rite, protected boundaries, taboo triggers, breach repair, and release from vows.
- **Variation:** devotional, courtly, occult, contractual, seasonal, secret-society.
- **Failure modes:** generic mysticism, rules bent for convenience, taboo without social or supernatural consequence.
- **Boundaries:** separate fictional ritual from real-world claims; define blasphemy, coercion, blood/injury, and public participation limits.
- **Media application:** visual media benefit from repeated symbols; RP needs exact ritual state; games can make sequence and consequence interactive.

## Bond Exclusivity and Jealousy

- **Payload:** attachment made meaningful through a declared relationship configuration, chosen priority, and the risk of displaced attention.
- **Scene grammar:** establish configuration -> allocate a scarce relational resource -> trigger jealousy or reassurance -> negotiate behavior -> leave residue.
- **Consistency rules:** distinguish romance, intimacy, ritual, domestic priority, and ordinary friendship; do not let jealousy create permissions that were never granted.
- **Relationship constants:** record mutual/asymmetric/plural/open/unresolved status, explicit allowance or exclusion of betrayal/NTR/partner sharing, jealousy triggers, scarce resources, disclosure duties, and exit conditions.
- **Variation:** public/private recognition, status rituals, time allocation, titles, route exclusivity, negotiated compersion, temporary uncertainty.
- **Failure modes:** inferring exclusivity from intensity, treating jealousy as ownership, adding harem/NTR because the cast is plural, resolving every conflict through reassurance.
- **Boundaries:** adult consenting participants, explicit opt-in to betrayal/NTR or partner-sharing fantasy, no surveillance or punishment beyond negotiated scope.
- **Media application:** RP asks once when configuration is missing; route games isolate branch knowledge; drama externalizes scarce time/status; ensemble media track who knows the contract.

## Corruption and Value Drift

- **Payload:** chosen fascination with a person's values, habits, loyalties, or self-story changing under sustained pressure.
- **Scene grammar:** baseline value -> tempting exception -> knowingly repeated choice -> visible rationalization -> consequence and possible resistance.
- **Consistency rules:** define which values may change, which identity anchors remain protected, what evidence marks change, and whether reversal is possible.
- **Relationship constants:** record who may invite change, explicit opt-in, protected values, non-reversible points if any, jealousy or loyalty triggers, pause/recovery paths, and relationship exit.
- **Variation:** mutual drift, role-specific temptation, public virtue/private exception, reversible performance, fixed-narrative fall.
- **Failure modes:** mindless personality replacement, treating consent as consent to every later step, skipping intermediate choices, forcing an eventual fall in Emergent RP.
- **Boundaries:** adults, explicit opt-in to the chosen change domain, no forced identity erasure, and no pre-scripted corruption outcome in Emergent RP.
- **Media application:** RP uses conditional thresholds; fixed narratives may plan an arc; games expose reversible choices and costs; visual media preserve pre/post identity anchors.

## Submission, Conquest, and Surrender

- **Payload:** a meaningful transfer of control or acknowledged defeat in which the ability to refuse remains legible.
- **Scene grammar:** challenge or request -> terms -> active resistance/choice -> bounded surrender -> check-in -> changed access or status.
- **Consistency rules:** define granted authority, duration, revocation, public/private scope, and what surrender does not transfer.
- **Relationship constants:** record exclusivity of the dynamic, permitted authority, forbidden commands, irreversible points, jealousy triggers, repair after overreach, and termination conditions.
- **Variation:** ritual, competitive, service-based, strategic, temporary reversal, private acknowledgment after public rivalry.
- **Failure modes:** conquest treated as blanket consent, resistance erased, surrender made inevitable, ordinary disagreement converted into authority play.
- **Boundaries:** adults, explicit opt-in, usable stop conditions, no intoxicated or coerced consent, no forced end-state in Emergent RP.
- **Media application:** RP requires agency-preserving gates; games need refusal and renegotiation paths; drama uses blocking/status; fixed narrative distinguishes authored defeat from player choice.

## Worship, Devotion, and Service

- **Payload:** desire expressed through attention, ritual care, praise, labor, or voluntary prioritization.
- **Scene grammar:** identify need or symbol -> offer service -> acceptance/refusal -> exact performance -> recognition -> residue or renewed obligation.
- **Consistency rules:** define whether devotion is romantic, erotic, religious, professional, or mixed; keep service character-specific and materially useful.
- **Relationship constants:** record exclusivity or plurality, protected outside duties, allowed titles/rituals, jealousy triggers, service limits, burnout signs, and release from obligation.
- **Variation:** mutual devotion, hidden service, public office/private reverence, playful ritual, reluctant recipient, role reversal.
- **Failure modes:** worship erasing the devotee's goals, endless praise, service becoming generic obedience, sacred framing overriding consent.
- **Boundaries:** adults, explicit opt-in to intimate or power-coded service, no real-world religious claims, and clear refusal/exit paths.
- **Media application:** RP varies service through practical scene needs; animation/comics use repeated gestures and props; games attach devotion to choices rather than passive meters.

## Fertility, Pregnancy, and Reproductive Fantasy

- **Payload:** explicitly chosen adult fantasy centered on reproductive possibility, pregnancy, fertility symbolism, lineage, or bodily/social after-state.
- **Scene grammar:** opt-in and scope -> establish reality mode -> possibility or symbolic act -> bodily/social consequence -> care, uncertainty, or aftermath.
- **Consistency rules:** declare realistic/symbolic/magical mode, participants' adult status, contraception or conception rules, timeline, reversibility where fictional, health abstraction, and world response.
- **Relationship constants:** record relationship configuration, reproductive exclusivity, parenthood or non-parenthood intent, allowed changes, jealousy triggers, non-reversible points, recovery/support, and termination options.
- **Variation:** symbolic fertility rite, planned conception, magical temporary state, lineage politics, nurturing after-state, explicit exclusion of parenthood despite the fantasy.
- **Failure modes:** surprise pregnancy added as escalation, treating consent to intimacy as reproductive consent, instant timeline jumps, forcing pregnancy or parenthood as an Emergent RP ending.
- **Boundaries:** explicitly adult, consensual, opt-in fantasy only; establish reproductive scope separately; exclude minors, coercion, deception, and unspecified forced outcomes.
- **Media application:** RP asks and tracks scope without presuming outcome; fixed narrative may author a declared arc; games need separate consent and consequence choices; visual media avoid reducing the character to a body state.
