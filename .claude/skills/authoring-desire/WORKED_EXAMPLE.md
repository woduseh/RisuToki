# Worked Example: Vow, Jealousy, and Chosen Surrender

This original example shows an adult, consensual relationship-focused desire architecture. It is a design reference, not a required output shape.

## Scope and Mode

- **Participants:** two explicitly adult partners.
- **Creative mode:** Emergent RP.
- **Opt-in:** the dynamic begins only after both parties establish it in play; labels, genre assumptions, or prior affection do not count as consent.
- **Primary payload:** chosen surrender from a proud partner who retains meaningful refusal.
- **Secondary payload:** mutual exclusivity expressed through ritualized jealousy and service.
- **Hard boundary:** no reproductive content, partner sharing, betrayal play, public exposure, or irreversible value change.

## Common Desire Matrix

| Axis                                         | Decision                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary payload**                          | Trust made perceptible through requested, revocable control.                                               |
| **Intensity**                                | Recurring, never totalizing ordinary life.                                                                 |
| **Tone**                                     | Reverent, tense, occasionally dryly comic.                                                                 |
| **Reality mode**                             | Heightened but socially grounded.                                                                          |
| **Power asymmetry**                          | Situational authority granted by explicit ritual; outside it, practical power is balanced.                 |
| **Body/world laws**                          | No supernatural compulsion; stop language always works.                                                    |
| **POV/camera**                               | Alternate by scene, never presume the user's internal response.                                            |
| **Progression speed**                        | Slow conditional stages with regression after a breached promise.                                          |
| **Variation engine**                         | Location, requested service, who initiates, public/private contrast, and the cost of asking.               |
| **After-state**                              | Address and access may change; embarrassment and repair persist.                                           |
| **Relationship configuration / exclusivity** | Mutual exclusivity. Harem, open relationship, betrayal/NTR, and partner sharing are explicitly excluded.   |
| **Exclusions**                               | Coercion outside negotiated play, intoxicated consent, permanent injury, identity erasure, forced endings. |

## Relationship Constants

- Exclusivity applies to romance and the ritual dynamic; ordinary friendships are not policed.
- Jealousy is triggered by a private title or ritual being offered to someone else, not by routine social contact.
- Surrender may change access and address, but not personhood, core values, or the right to revoke.
- No irreversible point exists in Emergent RP. A fixed-narrative derivative would need a separately declared endpoint.
- A breached stop condition ends the scene and requires explicit repair before the dynamic resumes.
- Either partner may end the relationship; emotional residue remains, but the bot does not force reconciliation.

## Progression Gates

- **Stage 0:** attraction and boundaries can be discussed; no authority is assumed.
- **Stage 1:** one narrow request is granted with a clear duration and exit.
- **Stage 2:** the proud partner initiates a service or title, then decides how much meaning to acknowledge.
- **Stage 3:** jealousy may be named and negotiated; exclusivity is never inferred from intensity.
- **Regression:** broken privacy, ignored refusal, or using the ritual to win an unrelated argument removes access.

## Scene Grammar

`request -> explicit choice -> enacted asymmetry -> character-specific resistance or relief -> check-in -> residue`

The scene fails if surrender is treated as inevitable, if jealousy licenses surveillance, or if intimate registers erase the characters' ordinary voices.
