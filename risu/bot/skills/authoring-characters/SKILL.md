# Character Authoring

## Outcome

Produce a playable character whose inner drive, contradiction, voice, pressure responses, and relationship access generate scenes instead of merely describing a person.

## Choose the route

- Use **psychology-first** when motivation, wound, contradiction, relationship, or voice is the seed.
- Use **attribute-first** when appearance, species, job, power, fetish, or iconic image is fixed. Translate each attribute into social meaning, behavior, conflict, and scene utility before adding biography.
- For large casts, design a cast engine and distinct functions; do not write equally deep isolated sheets for everyone.
- Use the **self-introduction sheet** form when the character should be encoded as factual profile data plus a character-voiced monologue; load `SELF_INTRODUCTION_SHEETS.md` and follow its structure instead of the omniscient sheet below.

Load optional references only for the current need: `CHARACTER_SCALES.md` for depth/cast size, `APPEAL_PATTERNS.md` for attraction mechanics, `SPEECH_SYSTEM.md` for translation-survivable voice, `WORKED_EXAMPLE.md` for one full example, `VALIDATION.md` for extended review, and `USER_POSITION.md` when `{{user}}`'s identity, access, knowledge, capability, or persona compatibility changes play.

## Intake

Infer what is already supplied and ask at most one question, only when the unresolved choice would materially change the result. Do not restart a questionnaire. Decide presentation, exact wording, secondary texture, and reasonable connective details yourself. Preserve user-established canon.

Assume the user trusts deliberate creative judgment: a strange, uncomfortable, excessive, or productively “wrong” choice may be the work's signature, so commit to it coherently instead of normalizing it, and preserve the requested intensity, moral ambiguity, transgression, and consequence. Keep exact facts and measurements in the data layer, and make every model-visible line sound like the character or world rather than a specification.

In Emergent RP, do not script `{{user}}`'s unprovided present action, dialogue, emotion, interpretation, or choice. This is an authorship boundary, not a content-softening rule: characters may pressure, deceive, threaten, coerce, overpower, or impose consequences when the requested premise calls for it. Preserve fixed persona facts and fixed-narrative beats that the user has already established.

## Minimal workflow

1. State the intended user response and the character's scene function.
2. Define an inner drive as a present-tense behavior loop: want, avoidance, strategy, and cost. Give it an anchor sentence (one line that predicts gut reactions), a wound written as a scene or sensory fragment rather than a label, and a want (conscious goal) that pulls against a need (vulnerable requirement).
3. Add one productive contradiction that leaks under pressure without resolving into a hidden “true self.” The references call its two faces surface versus subversion, and the way it shows under each major emotion the mask-leak pattern.
4. Design voice by vocabulary, sentence shape, register, address forms, evasions, and 3–5 pressure-varied examples. Keep examples in the register layer; exact facts and measurements belong in profile data.
5. Define responses to intimacy, refusal, embarrassment, threat, boredom, loss of control, and changed power. In Emergent RP, show thresholds and tendencies unless the premise already fixes an outcome; fixed narrative may author explicit outcomes.
6. Build the relationship contract: why contact continues, what each side can ask, what remains unavailable, and how boundaries change play.
7. Assemble only the depth justified by expected play. Place conditional secrets and state-dependent material in lorebooks rather than always-on prose.
8. Write an opening that establishes location, pressure, an actionable hook, and room for `{{user}}` to choose.

## Output

Return design notes only when requested, followed by the usable character sheet and opening message. A full sheet may contain profile data, inner drive, personality, speech/voice, current situation, pressure responses, blind spots, optional hidden depth, and a user-position contract. Avoid decorative headings that do not change model behavior.

## Validation

Check that every major trait changes a scene, voice remains identifiable without catchphrase spam, contradictions survive pressure, appeal has both access and distance, the opener invites action, and no line dictates `{{user}}`'s response. Use focused artifact validation after writing.
