# Visual Identity

Visual identity must remain recognizable before rendering detail, lore, or text.

## 1. Silhouette

Define the character as 3–5 large masses:

- head/hair or headgear
- shoulder/torso shape
- lower-body proportion
- signature extension: coat, weapon, tail, wings, hair mass
- negative-space pattern

Test in solid black. If the silhouette requires facial detail to identify the character, it is not finished.

## 2. Shape Language

Choose one primary and one counter-shape:

- circles: warmth, softness, repetition
- squares: stability, duty, weight
- triangles: speed, danger, ambition
- verticals: authority, austerity
- horizontals: calm, heaviness
- asymmetry: instability, improvisation, lived history

The counter-shape should express the productive contradiction, not random decoration.

## 3. Three-Color Hierarchy

Assign:

1. dominant field color
2. structural secondary
3. high-value accent

Record material and light behavior where relevant. "Red" is weaker than "matte oxblood cloth with one lacquered vermilion accent."

## 4. Head and Face Anchor

Pick one feature readable in closeup and SD form:

- hair silhouette or part
- eye/brow geometry
- headgear
- ear/horn shape
- facial mark
- fixed expression asymmetry

Avoid relying on eye color alone.

## 5. Signature Prop

A prop must do at least two jobs:

- identifies role
- changes pose or movement
- carries relationship/history
- supports action/gameplay
- survives as merchandise

If the prop can be removed without changing a scene or silhouette, it is decoration.

## 6. Motion and Pose Grammar

Define:

- resting pose
- approach/entrance
- action silhouette
- defensive or embarrassed posture
- victory/failure posture

Motion should reveal character. Two swordswomen need different acceleration, recovery, and use of space.

## 7. Effects and Transformation

Effects need a grammar:

- source point
- path or geometry
- color/material behavior
- sound implication
- residue after use
- escalation rule

Transformation should preserve at least two pre-transform anchors and add one unmistakable transformed anchor.

## 8. Costume System

Separate:

- **invariants:** silhouette, motif location, signature prop, head anchor
- **variant slots:** season, role, rarity, disguise, formalwear
- **forbidden substitutions:** changes that make the design read as another archetype

Alternate costumes should rhyme with the base, not merely share colors.

## 9. Cast Contrast

Audit the lineup for:

- height and mass rhythm
- silhouette spacing
- palette ownership
- prop category
- motion tempo
- face/head anchors
- visual hierarchy matching dramatic hierarchy

Every member needs one individual anchor and one faction/family echo.

## 10. Reduction Tests

- **Silhouette:** solid black at thumbnail scale.
- **Three-color:** remove texture and shading.
- **SD:** compress to super-deformed proportions.
- **32px icon:** face/head anchor plus one accent.
- **Group silhouette:** identify each member without internal detail.
- **Costume variant:** change outfit while preserving recognition.

Failure in one test does not invalidate the design, but the intended product surfaces must pass.
