# Comedy Craft

Load this reference when comedy is a recurring scene engine, ensemble rhythm, or character-appeal mechanism. It is not a separate primary skill.

## Scene Functions

**Boke** and **tsukkomi** are scene functions, not permanent personality labels. The same character may create the false premise in one scene, expose it in another, and become the emotional residue afterward. Assign the function beat by beat:

- premise-maker
- straight reader
- escalator
- delayed witness
- consequence-bearer

Stable tendencies are useful, but a cast becomes flat when one person can only be foolish and another can only correct them.

## Five-Beat Construction

Design a comic unit as:

`setup -> beat -> heightening -> callback -> residue`

- **Setup:** establish an ordinary rule, expectation, or power relation.
- **Beat:** break or misread it once, clearly.
- **Heightening:** increase the cost, audience, precision, or commitment.
- **Callback:** return after enough distance for recognition; do not repeat the same sentence as a substitute for construction.
- **Residue:** leave a changed relationship, damaged object, new rumor, debt, embarrassment, or tactical consequence.

Not every scene needs all five beats on screen. The sequence is a planning grammar, not a quota.

Specific callback chains are examples, not future scripts. In Emergent RP, preserve the recognizable premise and variation rule while allowing the location, participant, escalation, and consequence to arise from the current scene.

## Running Gags

On every recurrence, vary at least one:

- **Location:** the same behavior collides with a new institution or physical constraint.
- **Power relation:** the target now has leverage, an audience, or authority.
- **Outcome:** success becomes worse than failure, or a former victim benefits.

Preserve the recognizable premise while changing the route and consequence. A repeated catchphrase without changed context is a reminder, not a developed running gag.

## Seriousness Protection

Comedy may reveal competence, pain, danger, affection, or denial; it must not erase them.

- Do not make a capable character incompetent whenever a laugh is needed.
- Do not reset injuries, promises, humiliation, or public consequences after the punchline.
- Do not interrupt every sincere beat. Place relief after pressure has registered.
- Let a failed joke, refusal to joke, or mistimed callback become character information.

## Validation

- **Role reversal:** can the usual straight reader become the premise-maker without losing identity?
- **Callback interval:** did enough scene information occur for recognition to feel earned?
- **Fatigue:** has the gag repeated without a change in location, power relation, or outcome?
- **Tension placement:** does relief release pressure, or prevent pressure from ever existing?
- **Residue:** what is different after the joke?
- **Competence hold:** does comedy preserve each character's established ability and stakes?
