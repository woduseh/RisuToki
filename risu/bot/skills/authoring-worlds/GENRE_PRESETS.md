# Genre Presets

Starting frames for worlds built on recognizable genres. Each preset gives default pressures, norms/taboos, knowledge horizon, the model's likely default drift, texture starters, and scene generators.

**Presets are launch pads, not finished worlds.** Use them to ride a genre deliberately: keep the expected beats, raise the texture resolution, and place one signature deviation. Every preset still passes the same validation as a from-scratch world.

---

## 1. Modern Academy / School (학원물)

- **Default pressures:** visible hierarchy (grades, clubs, looks, money), surveillance by peers, the calendar as fate — exams, festivals, breaks structure everything; reputation moves faster than truth.
- **Norms & taboos:** seating geography as social map; who eats lunch with whom is a public statement; confessing feelings is a semi-formal ritual with witnesses risk; teachers are environmental hazards more than characters.
- **Knowledge horizon:** students misjudge adult institutions; money and career consequences are abstractions; the school feels permanent.
- **Default drift:** generic Japanese high school with no specific region, era, or institutional personality. Anchor the school itself: its one weird tradition, its decaying building, what it is locally famous for.
- **Texture starters:** chalk dust vs. tablet screens (pick an era), indoor shoes, the sound of a specific club practicing through windows, convenience store after school as neutral territory.
- **Scene generators:** committee work forcing enemies together; rumor traced to its source; festival prep all-nighter; rooftop/stairwell confrontation; the empty classroom after everyone left.

## 2. Romance Fantasy (로판)

- **Default pressures:** rank determines speech, marriage is politics, reputation is a currency women trade in because they are barred from others; the social season as battlefield.
- **Norms & taboos:** unchaperoned contact is scandal fuel; a dance request is a public move; servants see everything and are treated as furniture (they are not); duels/honor mechanics for men, salon warfare for women.
- **Knowledge horizon:** no germ theory unless declared; inheritance law and annulment rules should be _specified_ because plot runs on them.
- **Regression / possession / transmigration conventions (회귀·빙의·환생):** if used, define the meta-rules as a hard system — what the protagonist remembers, what knowledge is usable, whether the "original story" exerts pull (correction force), and who else might be aware. These are world rules, not character quirks; put them in System Logic.
- **Default drift:** vague 19th-century Europe with modern dialogue. Pick the actual tech level, the actual church power, the actual legal status of women — the genre's drama lives in those constraints being _real_.
- **Texture starters:** corset economics (who can breathe is who can act), candle vs. gas vs. mage-light as class markers, the sound of carriage wheels announcing rank before the door opens.
- **Scene generators:** a dance card maneuver; tea as interrogation; the engagement announcement that ruins someone; servants' staircase intelligence; a duel everyone pretends not to know about.

## 3. Murim / Wuxia (무협)

- **Default pressures:** sect loyalty above blood, debts of grace and grudge (은원) compounding across generations, cultivation realm as caste, the 강호 as a parallel society with its own law.
- **Norms & taboos:** hospitality codes; the weight of a master's name; learning another sect's art without permission is theft of the gravest kind; face (체면) as a resource men kill over.
- **Knowledge horizon:** ordinary people know the martial world exists and avoid it; government writ stops at its edge; medicine is meridian-based.
- **Default drift:** power-scaling shounen with Chinese names. The genre's actual engine is _obligation_ — who owes whom what — not tournament arcs. Make the debts specific and the cultivation costly.
- **Texture starters:** calluses read like résumés, inns as neutral ground with unspoken rules, the specific sound of a sword being _not quite_ drawn, qi sickness as visible aging.
- **Scene generators:** a grace-debt called in at the worst time; sect politics at a funeral; an inherited grudge meeting its innocent target; a manual fragment surfacing; protecting a commoner who must not learn why.

## 4. Dungeon / Game-System World (던전·시스템물)

- **Default pressures:** the System as inscrutable institution (who made it? — usually unanswerable, always feared), gate/dungeon appearance as natural disaster, ranked licenses determining economy and class.
- **Norms & taboos:** stat privacy (asking someone's level = asking their salary), guild contracts as feudal bonds, loot law as the most litigated field in existence.
- **Knowledge horizon:** inhabitants treat game mechanics as physics — nobody says "RPG"; they say "that's how mana works." Block out-of-world gaming vocabulary unless the bot is explicitly meta.
- **System Logic requirement:** stat windows, skills, and levels are _data-layer_ material — use exact numbers and exact costs. Pair with `authoring-scenarios` for gate events, and `writing-lorebooks` + CBS for live stat tracking.
- **Default drift:** Solo-Leveling-shaped power fantasy with interchangeable dungeons. Anchor dungeons with ecology and economics: who sells torches, who buys monster parts, what a "safe floor" town smells like.
- **Texture starters:** the queue outside a gate at dawn, insurance brokers at the entrance, the specific bureaucracy of registering a new skill.
- **Scene generators:** a raid posting that smells wrong; a low-ranker's skill suddenly mattering; loot-rights dispute mid-dungeon; the floor that doesn't match the map; a licensing audit.

## 5. Myth Retelling (신화 재해석)

- **Default pressures:** fate as enforceable law (prophecies are contracts), divine attention as hazard, hospitality and oath mechanics with supernatural teeth.
- **Norms & taboos:** names have power — using a true name is a move; guest-right and oath-breaking carry automatic cosmic penalties; mortals theologize incorrectly and that matters.
- **Knowledge horizon:** mortals know the gods exist but their information is garbled doctrine; define what the _gods_ don't know — limits on divine knowledge create the drama.
- **Adaptation discipline:** run the expectation ledger from `SKILL.md` step 2 before any other step. Audience canon, not source canon, is the binding contract.
- **Default drift:** Percy-Jackson-flavored modern quips or marble-statue solemnity, with no actual religious texture. Pick one: lived religion (offerings, festivals, fear) or institutional religion (temples, hierarchies, heresy) and make it ordinary life.
- **Texture starters:** the smell of burnt offerings as background air, which gestures ward which god, what people do _reflexively_ when thunder rolls.
- **Scene generators:** an oath worded carelessly; a god in disguise testing hospitality; prophecy interpretation as legal dispute; a mortal's bargain coming due; the festival that must not be interrupted.

## 6. Modern Urban Low-Fantasy / Occult (현대 이능·오컬트)

- **Default pressures:** the masquerade (what hides the supernatural and what it costs to maintain), institutional cover-ups, practitioners as a precariat — power doesn't pay rent.
- **Norms & taboos:** territory etiquette between factions; debts to nonhuman entities never denominated in money; civilians who see too much as a logistics problem.
- **Knowledge horizon:** define exactly how much the public knows (nothing / rumors / open secret) — this single dial reshapes every scene.
- **Default drift:** generic urban-fantasy noir narrator. Anchor in a _specific_ city's geography and economy; let the supernatural map onto real urban systems (zoning, transit, gentrification).
- **Texture starters:** wards that look like graffiti, the convenience store that serves both worlds, what supernatural exhaustion does to a day job.
- **Scene generators:** a masquerade breach to contain; rent due vs. ritual components; a civilian client who lied; turf renegotiation after a death; the entity that wants to renegotiate its debt.

## 7. Magical Girl (마법소녀)

- **Default pressures:** public ordinary life vs. secret duty, transformation access, collateral damage, team trust, an enemy system that personalizes conflict.
- **Norms & taboos:** identity secrecy; transformation witnessed only under specific conditions; civilian promises competing with emergency calls; discussing the source of power may itself be dangerous.
- **Knowledge horizon:** define who notices disappearances, damage, and repeated visual motifs; adults should not be uniformly blind without a mechanism.
- **Default drift:** generic friendship speeches and consequence-free weekly battles. Make power costs, cleanup, and civilian scheduling real.
- **Texture starters:** hidden repair kits in school bags, glitter-like residue that never vacuums cleanly, group-chat excuses written during alarms.
- **Scene generators:** transformation interrupted; civilian friend reaches the battle site; power upgrade demands a social cost; team member refuses purification; aftermath before an exam.

## 8. Mecha / War Ensemble (메카·전쟁)

- **Default pressures:** pilot scarcity, logistics, military hierarchy, machine compatibility, civilian cost, propaganda.
- **Norms & taboos:** maintenance crews hold practical authority; sortie priority is political; discussing replacement pilots is taboo around active crews.
- **Knowledge horizon:** pilots know cockpit reality but not strategic truth; civilians know war through edited images and shortages.
- **Default drift:** machines as costumes and battles as power-scale ladders. Define doctrine, supply, repair time, and what the machine cannot protect.
- **Texture starters:** hydraulic smell in ready rooms, personalized checklists taped over official labels, silence when a transport returns light.
- **Scene generators:** sortie with an untested replacement part; propaganda interview after failure; pilot/machine rejection; maintenance strike; rescue target conflicts with orders.

## 9. Idol / Entertainment Industry (아이돌·연예계)

- **Default pressures:** public image, training debt, group hierarchy, fan interpretation, schedule exhaustion, agency control.
- **Norms & taboos:** center position is public politics; private relationships create brand risk; staff labor remains invisible until it fails.
- **Knowledge horizon:** fans see curated fragments; performers know stage craft but not every business decision.
- **Default drift:** effortless fame and faceless adoration. Specify rehearsal technique, contract power, audience segments, and failure recovery.
- **Texture starters:** throat spray labels, choreo marks worn into studio floors, fan slogans heard through backstage walls.
- **Scene generators:** center reassignment; live mistake becomes a meme; rival agency poaches staff; fan meeting with hidden history; comeback concept divides the group.

## 10. Sports Drama (스포츠물)

- **Default pressures:** measurable performance, roster selection, injury, coaching philosophy, school/club funding, finite seasons.
- **Norms & taboos:** playing through pain has local moral meaning; starters and bench players occupy different social worlds; film review is confession.
- **Knowledge horizon:** athletes know their body and sport but may misread institutions, media, or life after competition.
- **Default drift:** victory through willpower alone. Make technique, matchup, fatigue, and recovery specific.
- **Texture starters:** resin, tape, laundry steam, scoreboard glare, the sound of an empty gym after elimination.
- **Scene generators:** role demotion; rivalry rematch under injury; tactical style rejected by coach; qualifier weather; teammate chooses career over final season.

## 11. Tokusatsu / Team Hero (특촬·팀 히어로)

- **Default pressures:** public destruction, color-coded team roles, transformation resources, monster recurrence, sponsor/state oversight.
- **Norms & taboos:** roll call and finishing moves are ritualized identity; civilians judge heroes by cleanup as much as victory.
- **Knowledge horizon:** the public may know heroes but not their internal command structure or transformation limits.
- **Default drift:** episodic reset with interchangeable monsters. Couple weekly threats to institutions and cumulative team strain.
- **Texture starters:** suit repairs, public safety tape around transformation sites, children trading inaccurate hero statistics.
- **Scene generators:** missing team color; monster targets a civic ritual; transformation device confiscated; rival hero franchise; victory damages public trust.

## 12. Monster Partner / Creature Collection (몬스터 파트너)

- **Default pressures:** capture/taming ethics, trainer reputation, creature ecology, league or expedition rules, partner autonomy.
- **Norms & taboos:** ownership language differs by culture; abandoning a partner carries social weight; certain habitats forbid battles.
- **Knowledge horizon:** humans misunderstand creature communication; creatures know ecological truths institutions ignore.
- **Default drift:** creatures as interchangeable loadouts. Give species needs, individual preference, and partnership negotiation.
- **Texture starters:** feed preparation, travel carriers, registration tags, claw marks on domestic furniture.
- **Scene generators:** partner refuses evolution; wild migration crosses a city; league rule conflicts with care; stolen egg; old partner meets replacement.

## 13. Space Opera

- **Default pressures:** distance, empire/rebellion, fuel and communication lag, species diplomacy, inherited war.
- **Norms & taboos:** ship hospitality, airlock authority, naming dead worlds, translation etiquette.
- **Knowledge horizon:** local populations know routes and rumors, not galaxy-scale truth; faster-than-light limits shape politics.
- **Default drift:** every planet as one biome/culture and travel without cost. Define transit time, infrastructure, and internal diversity.
- **Texture starters:** recycled-air taste, customs seals, delayed messages, gravity changes visible in posture.
- **Scene generators:** distress call arrives years late; neutral station hosts enemies; translation error becomes treaty issue; fuel choice strands refugees; homeworld myth disproved.

## 14. Post-Apocalypse

- **Default pressures:** repair, scarcity, memory politics, settlement trust, environmental hazard, generational difference.
- **Norms & taboos:** waste is moral failure; old-world objects carry contested meanings; maps are political assets.
- **Knowledge horizon:** survivors know local hazards but mythologize the old world; expertise is fragmented.
- **Default drift:** endless ruins and leather clothing. Build ordinary maintenance, food, childcare, law, and boredom.
- **Texture starters:** patched filters, seed ledgers, reused signage, weather reports spoken like prayer.
- **Scene generators:** water system fails; archive contradicts founding myth; caravan brings obsolete medicine; ruin salvage awakens ownership dispute; young people want to leave.

## 15. Cyberpunk

- **Default pressures:** ownership of body/data, platform labor, surveillance, corporate jurisdiction, maintenance debt.
- **Norms & taboos:** privacy is class-coded; firmware provenance matters socially; going offline signals threat or privilege.
- **Knowledge horizon:** citizens understand interfaces they use but not the full stack controlling them.
- **Default drift:** neon-noir wallpaper. Tie augmentation to rent, warranty, employment, accessibility, and family obligation.
- **Texture starters:** skin-safe adhesive, subscription warnings in vision, unofficial repair markets, quiet zones without tracking.
- **Scene generators:** implant license expires; memory evidence disputed; union strike blocks cloud access; family member inherits debt-linked hardware; offline district becomes valuable.

## 16. Horror / Mystery

- **Default pressures:** incomplete knowledge, escalating pattern, social disbelief, contamination of safe routines.
- **Norms & taboos:** communities develop avoidance rituals before explanations; witnesses disagree on what counts as evidence.
- **Knowledge horizon:** define what the threat knows, what investigators can verify, and which explanations are culturally available.
- **Default drift:** random grotesque events or omnipotent monsters. Give the threat rules, blind spots, and interpretable residue.
- **Texture starters:** repeated wrong sound, objects returned slightly displaced, local euphemisms, mundane records with one impossible gap.
- **Scene generators:** safe rule fails once; witness account contradicts footage; investigation harms someone; false solution comforts the town; monster imitates a social ritual.

## 17. Crime / Detective

- **Default pressures:** evidence access, jurisdiction, reputation, institutional compromise, victim networks, time.
- **Norms & taboos:** who may ask questions is class-coded; informants price silence differently; paperwork determines which truth becomes official.
- **Knowledge horizon:** investigators never possess author-level certainty; suspects know local pieces and protect unrelated secrets.
- **Default drift:** genius deduction from arbitrary clues. Make evidence chain, access, and alternative hypotheses visible.
- **Texture starters:** interview-room temperature, copied case files, court deadlines, neighborhood watch routines.
- **Scene generators:** lawful evidence is unusable; witness protects another crime; partner interprets motive differently; press names the wrong suspect; solved case threatens institution.

## 18. Family / Workplace Melodrama

- **Default pressures:** money, duty, hierarchy, reputation, caregiving, accumulated resentment.
- **Norms & taboos:** conflict travels through chores, schedules, seating, and delegated labor; direct confession may be less credible than behavior.
- **Knowledge horizon:** each member knows a different version of family/company history.
- **Default drift:** constant shouting or coincidence. Build practical dependency and recurring ordinary spaces.
- **Texture starters:** shared calendars, reheated meals, expense envelopes, desk ownership, holiday seating.
- **Scene generators:** promotion changes home dynamics; care duty reassigned; secret debt surfaces; family event collides with launch; resignation exposes invisible labor.

## 19. Visual Novel / Otome / Gacha World

- **Default pressures:** route availability, shared-canon mystery, affection/access gates, collectible factions, event calendars.
- **Norms & taboos:** private scenes have scarcity value; character variants imply alternate context; route knowledge must not leak across branches.
- **Knowledge horizon:** protagonists know only their route state; meta-awareness must be declared as a world rule.
- **Default drift:** palette-swapped romances and roster bloat. Give every route/unit a distinct access fantasy and world function.
- **Texture starters:** invitation items, route-specific locations, event costumes with faction echoes, menus treated as physics only when explicitly meta.
- **Scene generators:** route event collides with faction duty; low-rarity unit owns crucial knowledge; bad-end residue crosses a loop; event variant changes public role; affection gate regresses.

## 20. Regional Legend Fantasy (지역 전설 판타지)

- **Default pressures:** local sacred geography, oral variants, tourism or modernization, family custodianship, seasonal ritual.
- **Norms & taboos:** outsiders use the wrong name; ritual labor is distributed socially; telling a story can obligate the teller.
- **Knowledge horizon:** no single version is fully authoritative; elders, children, officials, and spirits preserve different variants.
- **Default drift:** generic yokai/folklore collage. Anchor language, landscape, livelihood, and the politics of who gets to interpret.
- **Texture starters:** shrine repairs, dialect names, offerings adapted to modern packaging, weather signs attached to a specific ridge or river.
- **Scene generators:** festival version conflicts with family version; developer disturbs a boundary; tourist performs ritual correctly for wrong reason; spirit rejects official heritage status; lost verse returns.

## 21. Isekai Transfer / Summoning (이세계 전이·소환)

- **Default pressures:** involuntary displacement, dependency on summoners or local sponsors, unequal knowledge between two worlds, legal and economic precarity, and the unresolved possibility of return.
- **Norms & taboos:** summoned people may be treated as guests, weapons, witnesses, property, saints, or immigration cases; modern habits can violate local status rules; asking about return may challenge the summoner's legitimacy.
- **Knowledge horizon:** separate what the transferred person remembers from Earth, what locals know about Earth, and what either side falsely assumes. Language access, literacy, documents, citizenship, and legal personhood must be declared rather than hand-waved.
- **Transfer / summoning distinction:** unlike regression, possession, or transmigration, the person crosses from one existing world to another and retains a real relation to the world left behind. Define who or what summoned them, the summoner's obligations, whether return is possible, and what each world can exchange.
- **Cheat and modern-knowledge limits:** specify the social owner, cost, visibility, and institutional response to any granted ability. Modern knowledge is bounded by memory, materials, infrastructure, labor, law, and missing intermediate expertise; it is not automatic industrialization.
- **Default drift:** effortless adaptation, universal translation, consequence-free cheat power, and modern trivia making the protagonist supreme. Make dependency, failed transfer of knowledge, local expertise, and return politics active.
- **Texture starters:** clothing that marks no recognized class, forms with no valid birthplace, mistranslated names, familiar gestures read as insults, a useless modern object kept as proof of home.
- **Scene generators:** summoner fails a promised duty; return ritual demands a local sacrifice; modern technique needs an unavailable supply chain; translation privilege is revoked; two transferred people remember Earth differently; legal status changes after a public feat.

## Using a preset

1. Pick the preset; read its default-drift warning first.
2. Declare ride or cross (`SKILL.md` step 10).
3. Place one signature deviation and write its Contrast line.
4. Run `SKILL.md` from step 3 (current pressures); the preset pre-fills steps 1–2, not the rest.
5. Validate as usual with [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md); presets earn no exemptions.
