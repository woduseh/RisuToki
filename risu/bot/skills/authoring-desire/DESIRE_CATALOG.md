# Desire Catalog

Use the common matrix in `SKILL.md` first, then load one or more families below. Combination should preserve a ranked primary payload; equal-weight payloads usually blur.

## Size and Scale

- **Payload:** continuously perceptible difference in scale, reach, power, and environment.
- **Scene grammar:** ordinary objects become logistics; attention changes weather/space; workaround creates intimacy.
- **Consistency rules:** exact ratios, landmarks, reach, sound, force, possible/awkward/impossible table.
- **Relationship constants:** who initiates or imposes scale-coded intimacy, whether protection/control is exclusive, jealousy triggers, whether reversal is permitted or impossible, and whether exit or size restoration exists.
- **Variation:** protector/vulnerable reversal, public/private scale, changing environment, temporary growth/shrink.
- **Failure modes:** scale drift, generic "very large" prose, impossible contact, power asymmetry forgotten.
- **Media application:** RP uses pinned constants; comics use landmark panels; animation uses motion/sound; games turn scale into navigation and interaction.

## Transformation and Body Change

- **Payload:** crossing from one embodied state into another and living with the difference.
- **Scene grammar:** trigger -> transition -> new capability/constraint -> social/body residue.
- **Consistency rules:** starting state, end state, duration, reversibility, retained traits, memory, cost.
- **Relationship constants:** who triggers or witnesses change, intended identity/relationship shifts, exclusivity implications, irreversible points, and whether the end state is restoration, acceptance, replacement, or permanent loss.
- **Variation:** voluntary/conditional, gradual/instant, public/secret, desired/ambivalent.
- **Failure modes:** state details reset, transformation becomes costume change, cost disappears at climax.
- **Media application:** prose handles altered perception; visual media need preserved anchors; games need state-specific verbs; RP needs explicit current-state tracking.

## Nonhuman Bodies

- **Payload:** attraction and intimacy shaped by genuinely nonhuman senses, anatomy, movement, and social logic.
- **Scene grammar:** difference first creates misunderstanding/logistics, then distinctive accommodation.
- **Consistency rules:** sensory range, communication, temperature, diet, sleep, touch, movement, capability limits.
- **Relationship constants:** personhood and communication baseline, relationship configuration, species-specific bonding claims, jealousy triggers, and separation or care obligations.
- **Variation:** near-human, animalistic, mechanical, spectral, collective, amorphous.
- **Failure modes:** human mind in a costume, anatomy changing for convenience, alien logic vanishing during intimacy.
- **Media application:** visual media emphasize silhouette/motion; prose/RP use dual sensory lenses; games map differences to mechanics.

## Power Exchange

- **Payload:** asymmetry—consensual, coerced, institutional, supernatural, or mixed—that carries real authority and consequence.
- **Scene grammar:** terms or imposition -> enacted control -> resistance, compliance, or override -> residue, escalation, or renegotiation.
- **Consistency rules:** source of authority, status of refusal, public/private scope, duties, duration, and consequences of overreach.
- **Relationship constants:** who controls whom, exclusivity and transferability of authority, declared limits or their absence, jealousy triggers, persistence, and end state.
- **Variation:** formal/informal, service/command, ritualized/playful, situational/reversal.
- **Failure modes:** generic cruelty without a specific power logic, authority evaporating outside intimate scenes, or coercion softening into negotiation against the premise.
- **Media application:** RP tracks current authority without writing `{{user}}`'s unprovided response; drama uses blocking/status; games may use reversible choices or locked states as declared.

## Restraint and Containment

- **Payload:** limitation, enclosure, anticipation, and controlled access, whether chosen, imposed, accidental, or inescapable.
- **Scene grammar:** preparation or capture -> constraint becomes perceptible -> response under limitation -> release, escape, transfer, damage, or permanence.
- **Consistency rules:** mobility, circulation and breathing consequences, duration, restraint mechanism, and whether escape, release, or monitoring exists.
- **Relationship constants:** who restrains or releases, whether the role is exclusive, what panic or stop behavior changes or fails to change, and the intended aftermath.
- **Variation:** symbolic, spatial, clothing-based, magical, technological.
- **Failure modes:** impossible physics, consequence-free constraint, state drift, or clinical reassurance replacing the intended sensation.
- **Media application:** visual media use composition and negative space; RP uses clear state; games make interaction rules match the declared degree of control.

## Sensory and Body Focus

- **Payload:** sustained attention to a selected sense, body feature, material, or repeated physical cue.
- **Scene grammar:** anticipation -> focused perception -> character-specific reaction -> variation in context.
- **Consistency rules:** exact visual/physical facts, sensory vocabulary, character attitudes, frequency ceiling.
- **Relationship constants:** who directs or forces attention, public/private scope, exclusivity if any, jealousy triggers, frequency, and whether focus can be refused or redirected.
- **Variation:** admiration, comfort, teasing, ritual, aesthetic fascination, vulnerability.
- **Failure modes:** repetitive inventory, anatomy detached from character, every scene forced toward the focus.
- **Media application:** prose varies sensory channel; visual media vary framing and motion; games attach the focus to interaction rather than passive inspection.

## Clothing and Object Mediation

- **Payload:** desire routed through uniforms, masks, footwear, materials, tools, or meaningful objects.
- **Scene grammar:** selection/dressing -> role or posture change -> object mediates distance -> removal/transfer gains meaning.
- **Consistency rules:** garment construction, ownership, wear rules, sensory material, role symbolism.
- **Relationship constants:** ownership and lending rules, who may dress/remove/adjust, exclusivity symbolism, damage limits, transfer meaning, and return or retirement conditions.
- **Variation:** ceremonial, professional, disguise, transformation costume, inherited object.
- **Failure modes:** outfit as disconnected tag list, clothing continuity errors, object replaces character.
- **Media application:** strongest in visual identity and merchandise; RP/prose must connect the object to behavior and memory.

## Absorption, Fusion, and Spatial Fantasy

- **Payload:** boundaries between bodies, identities, or spaces become permeable while continuity remains legible.
- **Scene grammar:** approach -> threshold crossing -> altered perception/control -> separation or stable new state.
- **Consistency rules:** who perceives, who controls, what persists, reversibility, spatial limits, memory.
- **Relationship constants:** participation and control status, exclusivity or plurality, identity anchors preserved or erased, irreversible points, whether separation is possible, and post-fusion obligations.
- **Variation:** cooperative fusion, shared space, protective enclosure, identity blending, environmental embodiment.
- **Failure modes:** unclear subject positions, arbitrary abilities, identity stakes erased, permanent changes reset casually.
- **Media application:** prose/RP need explicit POV labels; comics/animation need stable visual grammar; games need control-sharing rules.

## Identity and Role Change

- **Payload:** attraction produced by adopting, losing, exchanging, or performing a social identity or role.
- **Scene grammar:** role assignment or imposition -> behavioral pressure -> leak, adaptation, or erasure -> recognition, stabilization, or breakdown.
- **Consistency rules:** name/address, memory, social status, duration, who knows, and final-state conditions.
- **Relationship constants:** who recognizes or assigns the role, relationship configuration, identity anchors retained or destroyed, intended value drift, jealousy triggers, and whether exit or recovery exists.
- **Variation:** disguise, status reversal, persona, body-role mismatch, chosen title, double life.
- **Failure modes:** role treated as a catchphrase, original identity erased, instant adaptation without friction.
- **Media application:** drama emphasizes performance; games use role-specific options; RP needs address/formality states and regression.

## Ritual and Taboo Roleplay

- **Payload:** desire intensified by formal rules, sacred/prohibited context, repetition, and meaningful transgression.
- **Scene grammar:** preparation -> exact rite -> pressure at the forbidden edge -> consequence/after-state.
- **Consistency rules:** wording, sequence, authority, automatic consequence, permitted variation.
- **Relationship constants:** who officiates or participates, exclusivity/plurality of the rite, exact prohibitions, taboo triggers, breach consequences, and whether release from vows exists.
- **Variation:** devotional, courtly, occult, contractual, seasonal, secret-society.
- **Failure modes:** generic mysticism, rules bent for convenience, taboo without social or supernatural consequence.
- **Media application:** visual media benefit from repeated symbols; RP needs exact ritual state; games can make sequence and consequence interactive.

## Bond Exclusivity and Jealousy

- **Payload:** attachment made meaningful through a relationship configuration, priority, and the risk or fact of displaced attention.
- **Scene grammar:** establish or impose configuration -> allocate a scarce relational resource -> trigger jealousy, reassurance, punishment, or rupture -> leave residue.
- **Consistency rules:** distinguish romance, intimacy, ritual, domestic priority, and ordinary friendship; declare entitlements explicitly or leave them contested rather than inferring them accidentally.
- **Relationship constants:** record mutual/asymmetric/plural/open/unresolved status, inclusion or exclusion of betrayal/NTR/partner sharing, jealousy triggers, scarce resources, disclosure rules, and whether exit is available.
- **Variation:** public/private recognition, status rituals, time allocation, titles, route exclusivity, negotiated compersion, temporary uncertainty.
- **Failure modes:** inferring exclusivity from intensity, treating jealousy as ownership, adding harem/NTR because the cast is plural, resolving every conflict through reassurance.
- **Media application:** RP asks once when configuration is missing; route games isolate branch knowledge; drama externalizes scarce time/status; ensemble media track who knows the contract.

## Corruption and Value Drift

- **Payload:** fascination with—or an imposed process of—a person's values, habits, loyalties, or self-story changing under sustained pressure.
- **Scene grammar:** baseline value -> tempting exception -> knowingly repeated choice -> visible rationalization -> consequence and possible resistance.
- **Consistency rules:** define which values change, which identity anchors remain or disappear, what evidence marks change, and whether reversal is possible.
- **Relationship constants:** record the source of change, whether it is invited or imposed, retained and destroyed values, irreversible points, jealousy or loyalty triggers, and whether pause, recovery, or exit exists.
- **Variation:** mutual drift, role-specific temptation, public virtue/private exception, reversible performance, fixed-narrative fall.
- **Failure modes:** personality replacement without a mechanism, skipping intermediate evidence, preserving a redeemable core by reflex, or forcing an eventual fall in Emergent RP when no fixed outcome was declared.
- **Media application:** RP uses thresholds or premise-fixed states; fixed narratives may plan a complete fall; games expose choices or locked progression as designed; visual media preserve or erase pre-change anchors deliberately.

## Submission, Conquest, and Surrender

- **Payload:** a meaningful transfer of control or acknowledged defeat in which refusal is defined as possible, futile, forbidden, removed, or merely symbolic.
- **Scene grammar:** challenge, request, or capture -> terms or imposition -> resistance, choice, or override -> surrender or defeat -> changed access or status.
- **Consistency rules:** define the source and scope of authority, duration or permanence, whether revocation exists, and what surrender does or does not transfer.
- **Relationship constants:** record exclusivity, command scope, irreversible points, jealousy triggers, consequences of overreach, and termination or inescapability conditions.
- **Variation:** ritual, competitive, service-based, strategic, temporary reversal, private acknowledgment after public rivalry.
- **Failure modes:** conquest unintentionally softening into negotiation, resistance erased when it should carry dramatic weight, inevitability removed when it is the payload, or ordinary disagreement accidentally converted into authority play.
- **Media application:** RP tracks authority while leaving `{{user}}`'s unprovided response unwritten; games may use refusal, renegotiation, forced states, or locked outcomes; drama uses blocking/status.

## Worship, Devotion, and Service

- **Payload:** desire expressed through attention, ritual care, praise, labor, or voluntary prioritization.
- **Scene grammar:** identify need or symbol -> offer, demand, or compel service -> acceptance, refusal, or override -> exact performance -> recognition -> residue or renewed obligation.
- **Consistency rules:** define whether devotion is romantic, erotic, religious, professional, or mixed; keep service character-specific and materially useful.
- **Relationship constants:** record exclusivity or plurality, outside duties preserved or consumed, titles/rituals, jealousy triggers, service limits or totalization, burnout signs, and whether release from obligation exists.
- **Variation:** mutual devotion, hidden service, public office/private reverence, playful ritual, reluctant recipient, role reversal.
- **Failure modes:** worship erasing the devotee's goals when that was not intended, endless generic praise, service losing character specificity, or sacred framing changing its own rules for convenience.
- **Media application:** RP varies service through practical scene needs; animation/comics use repeated gestures and props; games attach devotion to choices rather than passive meters.

## Fertility, Pregnancy, and Reproductive Fantasy

- **Payload:** fantasy centered on reproductive possibility, pregnancy, fertility symbolism, lineage, or bodily/social after-state.
- **Scene grammar:** establish premise and reality mode -> possibility, act, or imposition -> bodily/social consequence -> care, uncertainty, exploitation, or aftermath.
- **Consistency rules:** declare realistic/symbolic/magical mode, participants' status, contraception or conception rules, timeline, reversibility where fictional, health abstraction, and world response.
- **Relationship constants:** record relationship configuration, reproductive exclusivity, intended parenthood or non-parenthood conflict, imposed or invited changes, jealousy triggers, irreversible points, and whether recovery, support, or termination exists.
- **Variation:** symbolic fertility rite, planned conception, magical temporary state, lineage politics, nurturing after-state, explicit exclusion of parenthood despite the fantasy.
- **Failure modes:** pregnancy added when it is not part of the requested payload, instant timeline jumps, consequences disappearing, or an outcome contradicting the declared mode.
- **Media application:** RP tracks the premise without inventing `{{user}}`'s present response; fixed narrative may author a complete arc; games align open choices or locked outcomes with the design; visual media follow the declared camera stance, including body-only focus when intended.
